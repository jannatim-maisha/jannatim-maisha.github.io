Warm Greetings!!!

<div style="text-align: justify"> 

I am Jannatim Maisha, a Ph.D. student in Computer Science and Graduate Research Assistant at <a href="https://www.gsu.edu/">Georgia State University</a>, Atlanta, USA, since January 2026. I work with Dr. Yoon Jae Lee in the Body Data Interface (BDI) Lab. Previously, I served as an Adjunct Lecturer at the Department of CSE, <a href="https://seu.edu.bd/index.php">Southeast University (SEU)</a>, from July 2024 to December 2025. I have completed B.Sc. in Computer Science and Engineering from <a href="http://aust.edu/">Ahsanullah University of Science and Technology (AUST)</a>, Dhaka, Bangladesh. My research interests are centered on Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning, Natural Language Processing, Computer Vision, Multimodal Learning and Signal
Processing. I have also been a competitive programmer, mainly using C++ and Python in programming contests, where I learned to break problems into smaller parts and solve each one efficiently.
<br><br>
My current research includes contactless physiological sensing with camera-rPPG and mmWave radar, AI-assisted literacy coaching, and wearable fNIRS/PPG sensing and smartwatch interfaces. <a href="/research/">Read more about my current projects</a>.

My thesis research focused on addressing challenges in low-resource Natural Language Processing (NLP), where I employed advanced techniques such as contrastive learning and adversarial training to improve fine-grained classification performance for languages like Bengali. Building on this foundation, I expanded my work across diverse areas of machine learning applications (including ensemble methods), computer vision (CRNNs, Vision Transformers, CLIP, YOLO, and SLANet), large language models (GPT-4), and signal processing (Fourier transform), as well as applying NLP and vision techniques to software engineering tasks.

Alongside these technical pursuits, I have developed a strong and growing interest in Human–Computer Interaction (HCI), human behavior, and cognitive processes, particularly in understanding how people perceive, interact with, and adapt to intelligent systems. I am deeply motivated by research that bridges computational models with human cognition—transforming signals such as attention, visual behavior, and intent into more intuitive, accessible, and human-centered interfaces. I embrace a creative approach to problem-solving, take great joy in teaching and sharing knowledge, and am a dependable individual with strong time-management skills, natural curiosity, and a sustained enthusiasm for learning.
